# What If Your Alarm Clock Could Rewind the Day?

9 min 15 s · 2.5D Pixel Art · English · dramatic · original score · 1920×1080, 30 fps

## The idea

Remi Hale, 24, has one job on a Monday: be on time for an interview at Halden Architects. She oversleeps, misses the
tram, blanks on the one question that matters. On the way home, in the rain, a watchmaker sells her a brass alarm clock
with a seventh knob: turn it before you sleep and you wake up to the same morning. Seven turns. After that, it's only a clock.

She practises Monday until it is perfect, and the perfect Monday is the one where her little sister Pip rides out at
6:14 p.m. to bring her a good-luck card. The film opens on that crossing, in the rain, under the amber beacons.

The rewinds run out faster than the Mondays. Whatever she changes, it happens at fourteen past six. The watchmaker:
"A day isn't a recording, miss. It's a promise. You only get to choose who it's for." On the last Monday she turns down
the final round, keeps her promise, and is standing at the kerb when the van goes by. That night she has one turn left,
and doesn't take it. Then it's Tuesday.

## Look

The town of Larkfield as a tilt-shift diorama; cut-away rooms (the flat, the office, the clock shop) and a table-top set
where the brass clock is a metre tall, so its knob, bells and counter window read in close-up. Characters are pixel
sprites at 2× detail. Rain, amber beacons and wet asphalt for the crossing; a warm lamp for the nights; dawn blue for every
repeated morning, the same framing each time so the loop is felt.

## Sound

A pizzicato clock ticks under every countdown; the clock motif (A-D-E-F, minor; A-D-E-F#, major) on glockenspiel;
Pip's own tune on flute; a "rewind" (swell, a falling glissando, a bright chord) at every turn of the knob; heartbeats
and climbing tremolo into the headlights, then hard silence. Voices: Microsoft neural voices (see CREDITS).
