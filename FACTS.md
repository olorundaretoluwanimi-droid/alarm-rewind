# Facts stated in the film, and their sources

| Line in the film | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "One of the first alarm clocks in America was built in 1787. It could only ring at four in the morning, the hour its maker got up for work." | Levi Hutchins of Concord, New Hampshire, built a clock in 1787 whose bell rang at a fixed hour, 4 a.m., to wake him for work. | Smithsonian Magazine, "The Inventor of the Alarm Clock Could Only Wake Up at 4 A.M." (2013); New Hampshire Historical Society |
| "About two in three people say they have felt déjà vu at least once." | A review of surveys found that about two-thirds of people report having experienced déjà vu at least once. | Brown, "A review of the déjà vu experience", *Psychological Bulletin* 129(3), 394–413 (2003) |
| "Your body keeps its own clock: about twenty thousand nerve cells, just above the place where your optic nerves cross." | The master circadian clock is the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the hypothalamus, about 20,000 neurons sitting just above the optic chiasm. | National Institute of General Medical Sciences (NIH), "Circadian Rhythms" fact sheet |
| "Left alone, it runs about eleven minutes longer than a day. Every morning, the light puts it right again." | Under controlled conditions the average intrinsic period of the human circadian pacemaker was 24.18 h (about 24 h 11 min); light is the main signal that re-entrains it to 24 h each day. | Czeisler et al., "Stability, precision, and near-24-hour period of the human circadian pacemaker", *Science* 284(5423), 2177–2181 (1999) |
| "Once you know how something turns out, it feels as if you knew it all along. Psychologists call it hindsight bias." | People told an outcome judge it as more predictable, and misremember their earlier predictions as closer to it ("creeping determinism"). | Fischhoff, "Hindsight ≠ foresight", *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance* 1(3), 288–299 (1975) |
| "Every night, your brain replays the day by itself. In rats, scientists watched brain cells repeat the routes of the day while the animals slept." | Hippocampal place cells that fired together while rats ran a route fired together again during the following sleep; in humans, hippocampal areas active during route learning are active again in slow-wave sleep. | Wilson & McNaughton, "Reactivation of hippocampal ensemble memories during sleep", *Science* 265(5172), 676–679 (1994); Peigneux et al., "Are spatial memories strengthened in the human hippocampus during slow wave sleep?", *Neuron* 44(3), 535–545 (2004) |

Fiction: the clock that rewinds the day, Remi, Pip, the watchmaker, Halden Architects, the town of Larkfield and the accident at the crossing are invented.
